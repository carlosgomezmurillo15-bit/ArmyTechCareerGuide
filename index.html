Assignment 01: Collection Plan + Initial Sources

Army to Civilian Technology Career Guide

Education, certification, funding, and career-transition resources for Army soldiers pursuing IT and cybersecurity careers.

Carlos Gomez | Information Curation | August 2026

Topic Organization Sources Tool Selection

01

Topic Statement

My collection will focus on technology education and civilian IT and cybersecurity career resources for U.S. Army soldiers. I chose this topic because I am an Army Reserve soldier studying Computing Applications, so I have a personal interest in understanding how military experience, education benefits, technical certifications, and career transition programs can work together. The intended audience is Active, Guard, and Reserve soldiers who are exploring technology education or preparing for a civilian technology career but do not know which resources fit their goals, experience, or available time.

The collection will include degree planning, military credit transfer, Army and VA education funding, IT and cybersecurity certifications, no or low cost training programs, military to civilian skill translation, technology career pathways, and transition opportunities that provide civilian experience. It will exclude general military recruiting information, benefits unrelated to education or employment, non technology career fields, advanced technical tutorials, exam study guides, and promotional lists that do not provide enough reliable information to evaluate an opportunity.

Included

  • Degree planning and military credit transfer
  • Army and VA education funding
  • IT and cybersecurity certifications
  • No cost training and career support
  • Military-to-civilian skill translation
  • Career pathways and transition experience

Excluded

  • General military recruiting information
  • Benefits unrelated to education or employment
  • Civilian careers outside technology
  • Advanced technical tutorials
  • Individual certification exam study guides
  • Unverified promotional lists

02

Proposed Organizational Structure

I will use a task based hierarchy organized around the decisions a soldier needs to make. This structure is more useful than organizing sources by format because visitors can begin with a question—such as how to pay for school, which credential to earn, which career fits their experience, or how to gain civilian experience—and move directly to the appropriate section.

1

Plan Education and Funding

Tuition Assistance, GI Bill benefits, school comparison, degree planning, and military credit transfer.

How can I earn a degree without wasting time or benefits?

2

Build Credentials and Training

Certification selection, Credentialing Assistance, no-cost training programs, and professional development.

Which credential or training option fits my career goal?

3

Translate Experience and Explore Careers

Military to civilian career crosswalks, technology roles, required skills, salaries, and employment demand.

Which civilian technology career matches what I already know?

4

Gain Experience and Transition

Internships, apprenticeships, work-based learning, career preparation, and employment support.

How can I prepare for employment before leaving service?

03

Initial Curated Sources

The following seven sources form the initial foundation of the collection. Each source is organized according to the proposed task-based structure.

Source 01 Education and Funding

Tuition Assistance Program for Service Members

Publisher: MyArmyBenefits — Official Army Benefits Website

Category: Plan Education and Funding > Tuition Assistance

Tags: Official, Army Reserve, Degree Planning, Funding

What it covers: This official Army benefits page explains Tuition Assistance for voluntary off-duty education, including eligible Army components, annual funding limits, semester-hour limits, ArmyIgnitED steps, and current policy requirements.

Why it is valuable: It gives the collection a primary source for how soldiers can fund college courses instead of relying on school advertisements or secondhand summaries.

Who would benefit: It is most useful to Active, Guard, and Reserve soldiers comparing ways to pay for an IT or cybersecurity degree.

Limitations or context: Rules, command-approval requirements, and dollar limits can change, so users must verify details in ArmyIgnitED or with an education counselor; the page does not evaluate individual colleges.

Visit the Tuition Assistance resource
Source 02 Education and Funding

ACE Military Guide: Find Credit for Military Learning

Publisher: American Council on Education

Category: Plan Education and Funding > Military Credit Transfer

Tags: Official Guide, JST, Transfer Credit, Degree Planning

What it covers: The ACE Military Guide is a searchable tool containing academic credit recommendations for evaluated military courses and occupations, with an option to upload a Joint Services Transcript for a personalized view.

Why it is valuable: It helps soldiers identify prior learning that may apply toward a technology degree, potentially reducing repeated coursework, cost, and completion time.

Who would benefit: It is especially useful to soldiers who completed AIT or other military training and are planning a college program.

Limitations or context: ACE recommendations are advisory rather than guaranteed credit; each institution sets its own transfer policy, and the training completion date must match the applicable evaluation period.

Visit the ACE Military Guide
Source 03 Credentials and Training

Army COOL: Credentialing Opportunities On-Line

Publisher: U.S. Army Credentialing Opportunities On-Line

Category: Build Credentials and Training > Credential Selection and Funding

Tags: Official, Certifications, Credentialing Assistance, IT, Cybersecurity

What it covers: Army COOL lets soldiers search industry credentials related to military occupations and civilian career goals while reviewing credential requirements, difficulty, promotion-point information, and possible funding through Credentialing Assistance.

Why it is valuable: It provides a stronger starting point for comparing IT and cybersecurity certifications than training-vendor marketing because the credentials are organized around recognized occupations and Army programs.

Who would benefit: It is useful to soldiers deciding which credential supports both military development and a future civilian role.

Limitations or context: A listed credential is not automatically the best choice or guaranteed funding; eligibility and annual limits change, and commissioned officers are not currently eligible for Credentialing Assistance.

Visit Army COOL
Source 04 Credentials and Training

Onward to Opportunity

Publisher: D'Aniello Institute for Veterans and Military Families, Syracuse University

Category: Build Credentials and Training > No-Cost Training and Career Support

Tags: No-Cost Training, Certifications, Reserve, Transition Support

What it covers: Onward to Opportunity provides no cost career training, flexible pathways aligned with more than 30 professional credentials and in-demand fields, individualized support, and job-placement assistance for eligible military-connected participants.

Why it is valuable: It adds a practical alternative to Army-funded training because it combines certification preparation with coaching and employment services rather than offering coursework alone.

Who would benefit: It is useful to transitioning active-duty members, parttime National Guard and Reserve members, eligible veterans, and military spouses pursuing technology careers.

Limitations or context: Eligibility, available pathways, cohort timing, and exam fee coverage vary by status and location, and the self-paced format requires participants to manage their own study time.

Visit Onward to Opportunity
Source 05 Career Exploration

My Next Move for Veterans

Publisher: U.S. Department of Labor and O*NET

Category: Translate Experience and Explore Careers > Military to Civilian Crosswalk

Tags: MOS Translation, Career Exploration, Salary, Skills, Outlook

What it covers: My Next Move for Veterans connects military classifications with civilian occupations and provides job tasks, skills, education, salaries, outlook, certifications, and openings across more than 900 careers.

Why it is valuable: It helps soldiers translate military experience into civilian language and identify preparation gaps before choosing a degree or certification path.

Who would benefit: It is most useful to soldiers who want a technology career but are unsure which civilian role matches their experience and interests.

Limitations or context: The matches are starting points rather than formal equivalencies, and national data may not reflect local markets, security clearances, or an individual's complete experience.

Visit My Next Move for Veterans
Source 06 Career Exploration

CyberSeek

Publisher: Lightcast, CompTIA, and NIST NICE

Category: Translate Experience and Explore Careers > Cybersecurity Roles and Labor Market

Tags: Cybersecurity, Career Pathways, Labor Market, Credentials

What it covers: CyberSeek provides interactive cybersecurity career pathways, workforce supply-and-demand maps, salary information, common credentials, skill requirements, and education and training-provider data aligned with the NICE Framework.

Why it is valuable: It allows soldiers to compare entry, mid-level, and advanced cyber roles while connecting education choices to actual employer demand and regional opportunities.

Who would benefit: It is especially useful to users choosing among roles such as security analyst, incident responder, governance specialist, or security engineer.

Limitations or context: The tool focuses on cybersecurity rather than every IT career, and job-posting data can lag or differ from hiring outcomes, so users should supplement it with current local job searches.

Visit CyberSeek
Source 07 Experience and Transition

SkillBridge Program Overview

Publisher: Official SkillBridge Program

Category: Gain Experience and Transition > Internships and Work-Based Learning

Tags: Transition, Internship, Apprenticeship, Work Experience

What it covers: The official SkillBridge overview explains how eligible service members can complete approved industry training, apprenticeships, or internships during the final 180 days of service while continuing to receive military pay and benefits.

Why it is valuable: It adds an experience-based pathway that can help soldiers build a civilian network, strengthen a resume, and test a technology career before separation.

Who would benefit: It is most useful to active-duty soldiers approaching transition and to Guard or Reserve members whose duty status meets applicable requirements.

Limitations or context: Participation is not automatic; it depends on timing, provider availability, service policy, and written command approval, and completing a program does not guarantee employment.

Visit the SkillBridge Program Overview

04

Tool Selection

Selected Tool: GitHub Pages

I selected GitHub Pages because I already have experience and feel comfortable building and publishing HTML and CSS websites with the platform. This allows me to focus on evaluating, organizing, and annotating sources instead of learning an entirely new curation tool. GitHub Pages also gives me control over the website's headings, categories, source cards, links, and visual design.

The collection will use a simple one page layout with navigation links to the topic statement, organizational structure, curated sources, and tool-selection explanation but can very easily update as we go. Each source includes its category, tags, annotation, and a direct link to the original resource and will be expanded towards the 15-2o sources.
